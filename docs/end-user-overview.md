# Overview (For Hydrologists & Disaster Management)

This page explains OpenFloodAI without any technical background. If you are
a developer looking to set up the project, see the
[Developer Overview](project-overview.md) instead.

## What is OpenFloodAI?

OpenFloodAI is a free, open tool that watches video from a fixed camera near
a river and helps you notice when the water conditions might need a closer
look.

Think of it as a second pair of eyes on a camera feed you already have. It
does not replace your judgment or your team's process — it just helps you
catch changes sooner and keeps a clear, organized record of what it saw.

## What can it do for you?

- Watch a video from a river camera and check whether the picture is usable
  (not too dark, not blocked, not missing).
- Mark one area of the picture to keep an eye on, such as a bridge pillar or
  a riverbank marker.
- Notice when that marked area looks different than it did before, and save
  a picture of the change for you to look at.
- Let someone on your team write down what they saw in the video, so the
  computer's observation and the person's observation can be compared side
  by side.
- Put together a simple report showing what was checked, what changed, and
  where the computer and the human agreed or disagreed.
- Keep a history of past checks so you can look back at previous reports.

## What it does not do

- It does not send public flood warnings by itself. Any warning decision
  stays with your team and your normal emergency process.
- It does not replace a hydrologist's or emergency manager's judgment. It
  only points out things worth a closer look.
- It is not yet proven to reliably detect real floods. It is a review tool,
  not a finished early-warning system.
- It does not require you to write code or use a technical command line —
  see the [Quickstart](end-user-quickstart.md) for the plain point-and-click
  version.
- It does not upload your videos anywhere. Everything runs on your own
  computer.

## Who this is for

Hydrologists, disaster-management staff, field teams, and anyone evaluating
a low-cost, camera-based river-monitoring tool who wants to try it without
setting up any development tools.
